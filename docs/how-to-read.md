# How to read this site

Every lesson follows the same six-part route, so once you know one page you know them all.

| Part | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| I. Why should I care? | What problem is this, and what do I need to know first? |
| II. What and how | What is the idea, and how does it work, down to tensor shapes? |
| III. Does it actually work? | What does the evidence show, and what does it leave out? |
| IV. Think like a reviewer | Why this design, what is new, and what could be questioned? |
| V. Beyond the paper | Where does it apply, what carries forward, and what could you try? |
| VI. Lock it in | Quiz, one-screen recap, glossary, references. |

## The fast path

Headings marked ⚡ form the **fast path**. Read only those and you get the core of the paper in about 15 minutes. The paper card at the top of each lesson lists them. Everything else is the deep path.

## Three voices, kept apart

The most important habit on this site is keeping track of *who is saying what*. Each voice has its own callout:

!!! paper "The paper says (§3.2)"
    Faithful to the paper's text, always with a pointer such as §3.2, Eq. 4, Table 2, Fig. 5, or App. B, so you can check it.

!!! code "The code does"
    What the released code actually implements, with a link to the file or function. Sometimes it differs from the paper, and that difference is worth knowing.

!!! take "Our take"
    Interpretation, critique, speculation, and brainstorming. The dashed border is a reminder that this is opinion, not something the paper claims.

Anything we found ourselves, such as follow-up papers located by search, is labelled as found by us, never as something the paper says.

## The learning prompts

!!! predict "Pause and predict"
    A question to answer *before* reading on. Guessing first, even wrongly, makes the answer stick far better than reading it passively. Answers sit in a collapsible right below.

!!! bridge "Speech bridge"
    An analogy to speech AI (ASR, TTS, CTC, self-supervised speech models) wherever it genuinely helps, including where the analogy breaks down.

??? deep "Deep dive"
    Derivations and heavy detail live in collapsibles like this one. Skip them on a first read; nothing later depends on them.

Interactive widgets let you move a slider or step through an algorithm and watch the idea change. They work on phones too.

## Difficulty

Ratings are personal: they describe how hard a paper is **for a reader who is intermediate in deep learning and strongest in speech AI**, not how hard it is in the abstract.

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 Warm-up | Home turf. Thin primer, straight into depth. |
| 2 Comfortable | Familiar neighbourhood; one or two new ideas. |
| 3 Stretch | A new subfield, but the math is manageable. |
| 4 Challenging | New subfield plus real mathematical weight. |
| 5 Summit | Dense theory or several new subfields at once. Budget a few sessions. |

## Your progress

Tick the prerequisite checklist on a paper card to note what you already know, and press **Mark as finished** when you are done. Both are saved in your browser only; finished papers show a tick on the homepage.

## Honesty rules

Every claim about a paper points to where it comes from. Numbers in charts are recreated from the paper's own tables, never invented. Figures from papers are not copied; every visual here is drawn fresh, and we link to the original figure when it helps. When a paper is unclear, the lesson says so plainly.
